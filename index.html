Two things are called a captive

Most advisers work on one. Different regulators, different economics, and usually different people inside your company.

The two, and what each one does
Captive finance
An in-house bank. Lends to your customers or your dealers so they can buy what you make.
Captive insurance
Your own insurer. Carries risks you understand better than the market prices them.

At Snap-on I ran both, supporting the same franchise network. Which means I can tell you whether the two should be talking to each other. In most companies they are not.

Where I work

Manufacturers and franchise or dealer networks large enough that a captive is a real decision rather than a tax idea. Aerospace and defense suppliers in Connecticut, pharmaceutical and medical device manufacturers in New Jersey, and the industrial base across the New York corridor.

I am not a broker, a captive manager, or a tax adviser. I do not form entities or place insurance. Nothing waits for me on the other side of a yes.

Is a captive worth studying in your situation?

Four questions, two minutes. It will tell you no if the answer is no. Nothing you enter leaves your browser.

Roughly what do you spend a year on the lines you would consider putting in a captive?
Over the last five years, how predictable have your losses been?
Is there a franchise, dealer or member network attached?
Could your organization commit to this for five years or more?
